Title:
Statistical Pitfalls in Casimir Force Spectroscopy: How to Avoid False Positive Signals

Authors:
Bruno L. Olivaes

Abstract:
Precision nanomechanical measurements of the Casimir interaction at sub-micron separations are increasingly audited for hypothetical short-range modifications of gravity and exotic forces. However, residual force curves are intrinsically non-uniformly sampled and contaminated by steep short-range baseline drifts. In this paper, we demonstrate how conventional data-processing choices inadvertently fabricate statistically overwhelming false positive signals or severely degrade experimental sensitivity. Specifically, we show that: (1) naive 3-sigma Gaussian thresholds applied to exponentially distributed periodograms flag up to 36 spurious peaks on pure white noise; (2) unphysical polynomial detrending across steep z^-4 short-range profiles induces Runge-type boundary ringing that generates an apparent oscillatory anomaly at lambda = 583.5 nm with an artificial significance of FAP = 1.49e-45; (3) cubic spline resampling on irregular grids (CV = 40.7%) destroys 57% of true signal variance at short wavelengths, degrading the 90% Minimum Detectable Level by a factor of 2.28; and (4) surface electrostatic patch potentials measured via Kelvin Probe Force Microscopy (KPFM) yield strictly monotonic power-law decays (P proportional to d^-2.29), proving that patch roughness cannot generate periodic residual modulations. Adopting a physically motivated inverse-power baseline collapses residual variance (chi^2/dof = 1.14) and restores the true experimental null result (FAP = 0.163).
