Title:
Spectral Analysis of Systematic Residuals in Casimir Force Measurements: Lossless Vector Re-Audit, Statistical Pitfalls, and KPFM Patch-Potential Metrology

Authors:
Bruno L. Olivaes

Abstract:
Precision nanomechanical measurements of the Casimir interaction at sub-micron separations are increasingly audited for hypothetical short-range modifications of gravity and exotic forces. Through a lossless vector re-audit of published dynamic-AFM residuals (Decca et al., N = 497 and N = 288; empirical noise floor sigma_P = 0.313 mPa, sigma_F = 0.229 pN), we demonstrate how conventional data-processing choices inadvertently fabricate statistically overwhelming false positive signals or degrade experimental sensitivity. Specifically, we show that: (1) naive 3-sigma Gaussian thresholds applied to exponentially distributed periodograms flag up to 36 spurious peaks on pure white noise; (2) unphysical polynomial detrending across steep z^-4 short-range profiles induces Runge-type boundary ringing that generates an apparent oscillatory anomaly at lambda = 583.5 nm with chi^2/dof = 3.74 and Baluev FAP = 1.49e-45; (3) cubic spline resampling on irregular grids (CV = 40.7%) attenuates 22.6% of true signal variance at short wavelengths (lambda = 7.5 nm), degrading the 90% Minimum Detectable Level by a factor of 2.27x; and (4) surface electrostatic patch potentials measured via Kelvin Probe Force Microscopy (KPFM) yield strictly monotonic power-law decays (P_patch proportional to d^-2.29), proving that patch roughness cannot generate periodic residual modulations. We further prove via first-order Taylor expansion that experimental contact-point calibration offsets (delta z_0) generate a spurious -4 C_4 delta z_0 z^-5 term analytically absorbed in first order by our physically motivated {1, z^-4, z^-5} baseline, collapsing residual variance (chi^2/dof = 1.14) and restoring the true experimental null result (FAP = 0.163).
